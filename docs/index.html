How this works
Meridian is a buy-the-dip dashboard for monthly investors. It does one thing: tells you whether now is a better-than-average time to put money into each fund, based on how far it has fallen from its recent high.

It is not a trading tool. It is designed for investors who make regular monthly contributions and want to know when to stick to their plan, when to add a little extra, and when to wait a month.

Tap any signal pill on a fund card to jump to its explanation in this guide.
Verdict levels
DEEP BUY
The fund has fallen significantly from its recent high — a level that, historically, tends to be close to a bottom. The selling pressure is starting to ease. For a patient long-term investor this is a strong entry point. Consider adding more than your usual scheduled amount.
BUY
A meaningful pullback. The fund is down enough that you are buying at a noticeably better price than the recent peak. Add as planned, or a little extra if you have spare capital.
BUILD
A moderate dip — real, but not deep. You are buying below the recent peak, which is better than buying at the top. Stick to your scheduled contribution. Not a signal to add extra.
HOLD
The fund is near its recent high, or it is falling and has not shown any signs of steadying yet. Either way, this is not an opportune entry. Stick to your scheduled contribution and wait for a better signal.

Whether the fund closed higher or lower than the previous month. A green signal after a period of falls suggests the selling may be losing momentum. A red signal while the fund is already down confirms the decline is still active.

Whether the recent 1-month trend is better or worse than the 3-month trend. When the fund is falling, green means the pace of decline is getting slower — an early sign the worst may be passing. Red means the fall is still accelerating. When the fund is rising, green means the upward momentum is strengthening. Red means growth is decelerating — the fund may be approaching a peak.
Floor holding Making new lows Daily
Whether the fund is still making new 3-month lows. Green means the current price is above the lowest point of the past 3 months — the fund is not in free-fall and a floor may be forming. Red means the fund just hit a new 3-month low — the selling has not yet found a bottom. This signal is specifically designed to be readable at the deepest dips, where other signals may still be negative.
Dip character
Sharp recent fall Daily
More than half the total dip happened in the last 3 months. A sharp, concentrated fall is more likely to be a market-driven correction — the kind that tends to bounce. It gives you more confidence that you are buying at a temporary low rather than a fund in structural decline.
Long-running decline Daily
The fund has been declining gradually over an extended period. Less than 15% of the total fall happened recently — meaning most of the decline is old. A slow grind lower is a less compelling buying pattern than a sharp short-term correction, as it can indicate structural weakness rather than a temporary dip.
Volatility easing Volatility rising Daily
Whether day-to-day price swings are getting smaller or larger. Green means the fund's daily moves over the last month are smaller than its typical daily moves over the past year — the market is settling down. During a dip, this is one of the earliest signs that selling pressure is exhausting itself and a floor may be forming. Red means daily swings are getting larger — the fall may still be accelerating and catching a floor prematurely carries more risk.
